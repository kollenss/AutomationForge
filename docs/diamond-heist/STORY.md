# STORY BIBLE – Operation: Le Cœur Bleu

## Background

**Paris, 1987.**
Viktor Beaumont – a corrupt art dealer – manipulated Marguerite's bankruptcy and took her most valuable possession: *Le Cœur Bleu*, a 14-carat blue diamond with unknown origin and immeasurable sentimental value. Beaumont laundered the diamond through a series of fake auctions and sold it to Musée Privé Beaumont – a private museum he founded in Paris, built as a monument to his thefts.

Marguerite appealed. Nothing happened.

Viktor Beaumont died in 2019. The museum is now run by his son **Édouard Beaumont** – equally greedy, equally ruthless.

**The diamond belonged to your grandmother. And now you know where it is.**

## The Planning Book

**OPERATION: LE CŒUR BLEU**
*Classified – Cardinal Eyes Only*

**Situation Analysis**

Musée Privé Beaumont has an older-model surveillance system – expensive but not unconquerable. Two cameras cover the entrance floor. One in the lobby, one in the corridor outside the server room. Both feed to the security control in the north corner of the floor.

The system has a critical weakness: it is not connected. No remote monitoring. Everything is handled locally.

That means when the security control is under our control – the entire floor is under our control.

**Team movements**

Ghost enters first. Non-negotiable. Before anyone else moves in the building, we need to know the entrance is secured and the guards are following their usual pattern. Ghost reports to Cardinal when the lobby is clear.

*Note: Cardinal does not confirm receipt until the report comes in. That's protocol.*

When we have Cardinal's confirmation, Wraith moves toward security control. Not a second before. Wraith works fast but requires that the corridor camera is already blind – otherwise the movement is visible. Ghost handles that from the lobby.

When the camera feeds are under Wraith's control, Circuit is free to move. Not before. The server room is directly under the camera's normal field of view and Circuit is good at many things – but not at being invisible.

The USB drive is in rack 3, position 7. Circuit knows which rack. Circuit knows which tool is needed.

**Technical access**

The terminal on the server floor requires physical access and authentication. Circuit's old access code from the installation – he noted it in the margin during fieldwork: BR-4471. The system should still accept it. Should.

**What can go wrong**

If the order breaks – if someone moves too early – the system flags a movement anomaly. We don't have the margin to handle that. Beaumont's security chief is not stupid. He's just not prepared for us.

Keep the order. Trust the team. Trust Cardinal.
